# 🔴 Read this before the paste — this one carries a real business risk

I looked at your product photo. This bag is a close copy of the **Louis Vuitton Neverfull**:

- the same tapered wide-top tote silhouette
- the same **brown canvas with tan trim** colourway
- the same **vachetta-tan handles** and side cinch laces with metal eyelets
- an all-over pattern built from **four-petal flowers and quatrefoil diamonds** — those specific
  motifs *are* the Louis Vuitton Monogram

The initials have been altered, but that is not what protects you. **Trade dress covers the
overall look** — shape, colourway, pattern, hardware — not just the letters. And your product
title uses the word **"Monogram"**, which is the actual name of Louis Vuitton's canvas line.

## What this can actually cost you — the practical risk, not a lecture

**1. Your Meta ad account.** This is the big one. Louis Vuitton runs one of the most aggressive
brand-protection programmes in the world and files automated reports through Meta's Brand Rights
Protection system. The usual outcome is not a warning — it is **the ad account disabled, the
product catalog rejected, and sometimes the Page removed.** You run Meta Pixel on this site, so I
assume you advertise. **You would lose the account, not just the ad.**

**2. Google Merchant Center** treats counterfeit as an account-level suspension, not a per-product
one.

**3. Your payment gateway.** Counterfeit-goods clauses are standard in merchant agreements,
SSLCommerz included.

**4. The ceiling it puts on you.** You told me the vision is *"a trendy bag company for women."*
A brand cannot be built on another brand's pattern — the moment Kohthai is worth noticing is the
moment this becomes the thing people notice.

## What I would do

**At minimum, today: take "Monogram Pattern" out of the title.** It is the single word most likely
to be matched by an automated brand-protection crawler, and it earns you nothing —
**nobody in Bangladesh is searching for "monogram pattern tote".**
