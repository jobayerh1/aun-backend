<p style="color:#475569;max-width:820px;margin:0 auto 26px;line-height:1.7;text-align:center;">Almost every projector brand — global names and budget brands alike — prints the brightness of the <strong>light source</strong> on the spec sheet. <h2 style="color:#0f172a;margin-bottom:8px;">How Professionals Measure Real Brightness</h2>
<div style="width:60px;height:3px;background:#0188fe;border-radius:2px;margin:0 0 14px;opacity:.85;"></div>
<p style="color:#475569;line-height:1.75;font-size:15px;">Professional reviewers and test labs don't measure the lamp — they measure <strong>the screen</strong>. The international standard (known as <strong>ANSI&nbsp;/&nbsp;ISO&nbsp;21118</strong>) works like this:</p>
<ul style="color:#475569;line-height:1.9;font-size:15px;padding-left:20px;">
<li>Project a pure white image in a dark room.</li>
<li>Measure the light at <strong>9 points</strong> across the screen with a lux meter.</li>
<li>Average the 9 readings and multiply by the screen area.</li>
</ul>
<p style="color:#475569;line-height:1.75;font-size:15px;">The result — often called <em>ANSI lumens</em> — is real, on-screen brightness. It is <strong>always dramatically lower</strong> than the light-source rating on the box, for premium brands and budget brands alike. That's why a projector reviewed at a few hundred ANSI lumens can carry a spec sheet claiming several thousand.</p>
<p style="color:#0c2a4a;line-height:1.7;font-size:15px;background:#f0f7ff;border:1px solid #bcd9f8;border-radius:10px;padding:14px 16px;"><i class="fa-solid fa-circle-info" style="color:#0188fe;margin-right:8px;"></i><strong>Rule of thumb:</strong> when you see a big lumens claim anywhere online, mentally cut it in half (or more) to estimate real on-screen brightness. Apply that rule to every brand equally — then compare.</p>

<h2 style="color:#0f172a; text-align:center; margin-bottom:10px;">Frequently Asked Questions &mdash; Projector Brightness</h2>
<div style="width:60px;height:3px;background:#0188fe;border-radius:2px;margin:0 auto 22px;opacity:.85;"></div>
<div class="aun-faq">
<details open><summary><span>Why does my projector look dimmer than the number on the box?</span><i class="fa-solid fa-chevron-down aun-chev"></i></summary><div class="aun-faq-a">Because the box number is measured at the light source, before the light passes through the panel, filters and lens — and that is true for virtually every projector brand in the world. Real on-screen brightness is always well below the rated figure. Your projector isn't faulty; the industry's numbering convention is just generous.</div></details>
<details><summary><span>What are ANSI lumens?</span><i class="fa-solid fa-chevron-down aun-chev"></i></summary><div class="aun-faq-a">ANSI lumens (standardised today as ISO&nbsp;21118) measure brightness <strong>on the screen</strong>: a white image is measured at 9 points with a lux meter, averaged, and multiplied by the screen area. It's the method professional reviewers use, and it always produces a much lower — but far more realistic — figure than the light-source rating printed on spec sheets.</div></details>
<details><summary><span>Are the brightness figures on AUN spec sheets real?</span><i class="fa-solid fa-chevron-down aun-chev"></i></summary><div class="aun-faq-a">The figures we publish are the manufacturer's official light-source ratings — the same convention nearly every projector brand uses, which is why we label them <strong>"Manufacturer Rated"</strong>. On-screen brightness is lower than the rated figure for every projector on the market, including ours. That's exactly why we never sell on numbers alone: every product page tells you honestly which rooms the model suits ("shines in dim or dark rooms — close the curtains in daytime") and shows real, unedited projection photos so you can judge with your own eyes before you buy.</div></details>
<details><summary><span>A marketplace listing claims 8,000 or 12,000 lumens for a cheap projector. Should I believe it?</span><i class="fa-solid fa-chevron-down aun-chev"></i></summary><div class="aun-faq-a">Be careful. Unbranded marketplace projectors often print numbers with no measurement standard behind them at all — a "12,000 lumen" no-name unit can look dimmer on the wall than a properly engineered projector rated far lower. Apply the same rule to every listing: ignore the number, look for real projection photos, real video demos, a real warranty, and a seller who will answer for the product after the sale.</div></details>
<details><summary><span>Can any home projector beat full daylight?</span><i class="fa-solid fa-chevron-down aun-chev"></i></summary><div class="aun-faq-a">Honestly — no. Direct sunlight washes out every home projector at every price, ours included. For daytime viewing: close the curtains, keep the image size moderate, and consider an ALR (ambient-light-rejecting) screen, which visibly improves contrast in lit rooms.</div></details>
<details><summary><span>So how do I actually choose the right brightness?</span><i class="fa-solid fa-chevron-down aun-chev"></i></summary><div class="aun-faq-a">Start from your room, not the spec sheet: how much light it has, how big you want the picture, and how far the projector sits from the wall.
