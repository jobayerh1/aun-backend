<h2>What Are ANSI Lumens?</h2>

<p><strong>ANSI lumens</strong> is a standardized brightness measurement method developed by the American National Standards Institute. It measures projector brightness in a controlled way using multiple points on the screen.</p>

<p>This gives a more realistic and comparable brightness value between different projector brands.</p>

<ul>
<li>Measured using a standardized testing procedure</li>
<li>More accurate for real-world comparison</li>
<li>Used by many established projector brands</li>
</ul>

<h2>What Are LED Lumens?</h2>

<p><strong>LED lumens</strong> (sometimes called marketing lumens) are not always measured using a strict standardized method. Different sellers may calculate brightness differently.</p>

<p>This is why you sometimes see extremely high numbers that do not reflect actual screen brightness performance.</p>

<ul>
<li>No universal standard</li>
<li>Numbers may appear much higher</li>
<li>Not always directly comparable between brands</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why Do Some Projectors Show 10,000+ Lumens?</h2>

<p>In many online listings, brightness may be described using non-ANSI measurements. These numbers can look impressive but may not represent real ANSI brightness output.</p>

<p>For practical buying decisions, always compare <strong>ANSI lumens</strong> when possible.</p>

<h2>How Many ANSI Lumens Do You Need in Bangladesh?</h2>

<p>These ranges are on the <strong>measured (ANSI) scale</strong> — the scale professional reviewers use, not the rated figures printed on spec sheets:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Dark bedroom / night use:</strong> ~300–800 measured ANSI is plenty</li>
<li><strong>Living room with some light:</strong> ~800–1,000+ measured ANSI recommended</li>
<li><strong>Office / classroom:</strong> ~850–1,000+ measured ANSI for clearer text and slides</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Important:</strong> spec sheets across the whole industry — including our own price list — show <strong>manufacturer-rated lumens</strong>, which are always much higher than measured ANSI values. That's not one brand cheating; it's how the industry quotes brightness. As a rule of thumb, mentally cut any rated figure in half (or more) to estimate real on-screen brightness — and apply that rule to every brand equally before comparing.</p>

<h2>FAQ – ANSI vs LED Lumens</h2>

<h3>Is 10,000 lumens better than 800 ANSI?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. If the 10,000 figure is not ANSI-rated, it may not represent real brightness. ANSI lumens provide a more consistent measurement standard.</p>

<h3>Which brightness spec should I trust?</h3>
<p>For reliable comparison between different models, ANSI lumens are generally more consistent.</p>

<h3>Does higher ANSI always mean better picture?</h3>
<p>Higher brightness helps in bright rooms, but contrast, resolution, and color performance also matter.</p>

<h3>Why do AUN spec sheets show "Manufacturer Rated" lumens instead of ANSI?</h3>
<p>Because only an independently certified measurement deserves the ANSI label. We publish the manufacturer's official rating — clearly labelled — and back it with real projection photos and unedited factory lux-meter test videos, so you can judge actual performance with your own eyes instead of comparing unverifiable numbers.</p>
