# The verdict: the factory claims it, and the price says it cannot be true

**材质：植鞣皮** does literally mean vegetable-tanned leather. So you did not invent the claim —
you copied it in good faith from the factory, and their English title says the same thing. That
matters, and I was wrong to imply the phrase came from nowhere.

**But look at the price on the same page: ¥55.**

That is about **৳750**, or US$7.70, for a finished bag — with free return shipping and a ¥5.5
delivery charge on top. Vegetable tanning is a **weeks-long** process in bark pits rather than a
day in a chrome drum, and it needs hides good enough to show without correction. **The leather
alone for a 24 × 14 × 5 cm bag would cost more than ¥55.** Then add the gold hardware, the
lining, the zip, the cutting, the stitching, the factory's margin and the platform's.

It does not add up, and it is not close.

**What is almost certainly happening:** on 1688, `植鞣皮` is routinely used to describe the
*look* — the matte, tan, saddle-leather aesthetic — rather than the tannage. The same listing
also calls the bag `手拎/手提` (handheld) while selling it with a crossbody strap, and gives the
name simply as "fashionable casual handbag" with no material in it at all. This is loose
marketing copy, not a specification.

## So what do you do?

**You are the seller to the Bangladeshi customer.** If she opens the bag and it is plastic, the
complaint is against Kohthai, not against Baoding Mojingmei. A supplier's page is evidence you
acted in good faith; it is not a defence, and it will not be what she photographs.

⭐ **You have the bags. Settle it in thirty seconds:**

1. **Follow the grain across the flat front panel.** Machine-embossed PU **repeats** — the same
   pore cluster recurring every few centimetres, like wallpaper. Real leather never repeats.
2. **Look at the underside of the strap or any cut edge.** PU is a plastic film on a **woven
   fabric backing**. Real leather is fibrous all the way through.

**My expectation is PU, on the price alone.** If the grain repeats, that settles it.

### If it is PU (expected)

Remove "Vegetable Tanned Leather" from the product title. You lose nothing real — nobody is
searching for it in Bangladesh — and you remove a claim that a single customer with a phone
camera could turn into a problem. `Material: PU Leather`.

### If the bag genuinely surprises you

Keep the claim, and ask the supplier to put **植鞣革** in writing in the chat so you have it on
record. Then make far more of it than a line in a Materials tab.

# Three other things this listing changes

**1. Your weight is wrong. The factory says 310g, your site says 330g.**
Use **310 g**. Small, but it is free to be right.

**2. There are TWO sizes, and you need to confirm which you bought.**

```
Small   24 × 14 × 5 cm   310 g     <- matches your listing
Large   30 × 18 × 7 cm   530 g
```

Your site's 24 × 14 × 5 matches the **small**. If you actually stock the large, every dimension
on your page is wrong. Please confirm.

**3. ⚠️ Your interior description may not be true.**

The factory says the interior is **大容量主袋 — one large main compartment.** Your description
says:

> *"the organized interior—complete with a dedicated zipper pocket, cell phone bag, and ID bag"*

And your feature icons claim a **Card Slot**.

That is the same problem as the material, in a smaller way: **open the bag and count the
pockets.** If there is only one compartment, the Card Slot icon has to go and that sentence has to
change. If the pockets are there, keep them — the factory spec table is clearly not exhaustive.

**Also worth a glance:** the listing's selected colour is *"Black silver buckle"*, but your copy
says **gold** buckle. Check your own product photos match the hardware you actually received.
