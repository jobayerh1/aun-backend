<h2 id="lumens">2) How many lumens do you need?</h2>
        <p><strong>Rule of thumb:</strong> darker rooms need fewer lumens; brighter rooms need more. For home theater, picture quality improves fast once you control ambient light.</p>
        <table style="width:100%;border-collapse:collapse">
          <thead>
            <tr>
              <th style="text-align:left;padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #eee">Room lighting</th>
              <th style="text-align:left;padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #eee">Measured (ANSI) brightness*</th>
              <th style="text-align:left;padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #eee">Notes</th>
            </tr>
          </thead>
          <tbody>
            <tr>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">Dark room / night</td>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">~300–800 ANSI</td>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">Great for movies; best contrast and color.</td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">Dim room / blinds closed</td>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">~800–1,000 ANSI</td>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">Good for mixed content; larger screens.</td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">Office/classroom lighting</td>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">~850–1,000+ ANSI</td>
              <td style="padding:10px;border-bottom:1px solid #f0f0f0">Clear slides/text; dim lights if possible.</td>
            </tr>
          </tbody>
        </table>
        <p style="margin-top:8px"><em>*These are on the measured (ANSI) scale that professional reviewers use. Spec sheets across the whole industry — including our price list — show <strong>manufacturer-rated lumens</strong>, which are always much higher than measured values, for every brand equally. Rule of thumb: mentally cut a rated figure in half (or more) to estimate real on-screen brightness. Full explanation: <a href="/projector-brightness-explained/">Projector Brightness, Explained</a>.</em></p>
        <p style="margin-top:8px">Note: Many models are <strong>4K supported</strong> (accept 4K input and downscale) but native resolutions are typically <strong>720p or 1080p</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="throw">3) Throw distance (fit your room)</h2>
        <p><strong>Quick math:</strong> Screen width × <em>throw ratio</em> ≈ distance. For many home models, throw ratio is ~1.05–1.5.</p>
        <ul>
          <li>100″ screen ≈ 2.2 m wide → distance ≈ 2.4–3.3 m</li>
          <li>80″ screen ≈ 1.8 m wide → distance ≈ 2.0–2.7 m</li>
        </ul>
        <p>Check each product’s specs for the exact throw ratio, then match to your room depth. If space is tight, consider a smaller screen or a short-throw option.</p>
[/ux_html]

<h2 id="screens">4) Screen & setup tips</h2>
[/ux_html]
[accordion]

[accordion-item title="Screens"]

<ul>
<li>Dark rooms: matte white fixed-frame or pull-down screens work well.</li>
<li>Bright rooms: consider a higher-gain or ALR screen, and control light where possible.</li>
<li>No screen? A smooth, matte light-grey wall works in a pinch.</li>
</ul>

[/accordion-item]
[accordion-item title="Sound"]

<p>Built-in speakers are fine for casual viewing; add a soundbar or bookshelf speakers for movie nights or larger rooms.</p>

[/accordion-item]
[accordion-item title="Streaming & apps"]

<p>Android/Smart models run YouTube and more. Netflix support varies — use casting or a streaming stick if needed.</p>

<h2 id="faq">FAQ — Projector Buying Guide (BD)</h2>
[/ux_html]
<div class="rank-math-faq">
<div class="rank-math-list-item">
<h3 class="rank-math-question">What resolution should I pick — 720p or 1080p?</h3>
<div class="rank-math-answer">
<p>For movies and general use, both work; choose <strong>1080p</strong> if you want extra detail on 100–120″ screens or sit closer. Many models are <strong>4K supported</strong> for input but not native 4K.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="rank-math-list-item">
<h3 class="rank-math-question">How many ANSI lumens do I need at home?</h3>
<div class="rank-math-answer">
<p>On the measured (ANSI) scale reviewers use — dark room: <strong>~300–800</strong>; dim room or some daylight: <strong>~800–1,000</strong>; office/classroom slides: <strong>~850–1,000+</strong>. Remember that spec sheets show manufacturer-rated lumens, a much higher scale — see our <a href="/projector-brightness-explained/">brightness guide</a> before comparing numbers.</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="rank-math-list-item">
<h3 class="rank-math-question">Will Netflix and YouTube work?</h3>
<div class="rank-math-answer">
<p>YouTube generally works out of the box. Netflix depends on the model/app policy; if needed, use <strong>casting</strong> or a streaming stick (Fire TV, Chromecast, etc.).</p>
</div>
</div>
<div class="rank-math-list-item">
<h3 class="rank-math-question">What’s a good screen size and distance?</h3>
<div class="rank-math-answer">
<p>Most users pick <strong>80–120″</strong>. Roughly, screen width × <em>1.2–1.5</em> ≈ throw distance. Check each product’s throw ratio for an exact fit.</p>
</div>
